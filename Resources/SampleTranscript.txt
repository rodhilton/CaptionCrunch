Alex: Before we start, we need to decide whether to follow the river road or cut through the old orchard.

Morgan: The orchard is faster, but the map says people hear bells there after sunset. I think we should ask Mara what she saw.

Mara: I saw lanterns moving between the trees. They were too high to be people walking. One of them stopped when I said the miller's name.

Alex: Then the immediate job is simple. We find the mill, learn why the lanterns are watching it, and avoid making a bargain until we know what they want.

Morgan: I wrote down three open questions: who rang the bells, why the miller disappeared, and whether the orchard path is still safe for travelers.

Mara: The bell I heard was not coming from the chapel. It was under the ground, somewhere near the dry well.

Alex: If the well is connected to the mill, that means the lanterns might be markers instead of warnings. They could be showing the safe stones.

Morgan: Or they could be bait. The last time someone followed pretty lights into old trees, we had to trade a silver button and three memories to get them back.

Mara: I still think the miller's name matters. When I said it, every lantern turned blue for a second, and the wind stopped.

Alex: Then we start with the name. We ask the baker, the stable hand, and anyone old enough to remember when the mill wheel still moved.

Morgan: I want someone watching the orchard while we ask questions. If the lanterns move closer to town, we leave immediately.

Mara: Also, the well had fresh mud on the stones. Someone climbed out recently, or something did.

Alex: New plan. We gather rope, chalk, two lanterns of our own, and enough food for one night. We do not enter the well until morning.

Morgan: And if the bells start before sunrise?

Mara: Then we wake everyone in the inn and make sure nobody answers their name from outside.

Alex: Good. Our next steps are supplies, witnesses, the miller's full name, and then the well at first light.
